# What this looks like for a legal team

These scenarios illustrate documented workflows. They are **examples**, not customer testimonials, measured productivity claims, or proof of any particular live tenant's configuration.

## 1. Review an incoming vendor agreement

**Situation:** Your client is considering a vendor's MSA with broad indemnity language and an aggressive limitation of liability.

**Workflow:** Import the PDF or DOCX, select the represented party, and run review issue spotting. Inspect each reported clause and the relevant firm playbook; choose whether to approve, seek negotiation, reject current language, request clarification, or accept an exception with a recorded rationale. Prepare a redline and compare the proposed changes before completing review.

**Value:** Legal judgment becomes a documented, reusable decision trail tied to the language that was actually reviewed.

## 2. Create a client-specific NDA from governed content

**Situation:** Two businesses need a confidentiality agreement, with special attention to exceptions, permitted disclosures, ownership and duration.

**Workflow:** Open the confidentiality library, answer guided questions and select the relevant drafting posture. Review issue cards and clause choices, assemble the draft from maintained language, address flagged missing terms, and move the completed draft into approval.

**Value:** The team uses governed starting language instead of relying on an unrestricted AI prompt.

## 3. Handle contract intake across a firm

**Situation:** Operations staff repeatedly request agreements that would otherwise arrive as unstructured emails.

**Workflow:** An authorized teammate completes internal intake, uploads any source document, and submits it. Counsel accepts the request, creating a draft in the organization workspace. The firm assigns review and manages the record from one location.

**Value:** Requests and the related legal work are connected to an organization-owned matter instead of a single employee's inbox.

## 4. Complete an approved signature process

**Situation:** A legal team needs to ensure that a revised agreement was approved before it is executed.

**Workflow:** Record approval for the current text. If the content changes, the approval gate blocks signature until the changed version is approved under the applicable policy. Store an executed copy and verify its integrity on subsequent downloads.

**Value:** Reduces ambiguity about which version was approved and which version was signed.

## 5. Preserve obligations after execution

**Situation:** A supplier contract contains notice duties, renewal terms and deliverables.

**Workflow:** Record the executed contract, allow configured obligation extraction, review the generated duties, and compute dates from the term facts. Track each duty's state and open the underlying source contract when needed.

**Value:** The signed file remains an operational record rather than becoming a forgotten attachment. **Important:** the reviewed release does not send automated deadline reminders.
