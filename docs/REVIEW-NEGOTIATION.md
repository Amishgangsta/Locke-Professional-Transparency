# Counterparty review and negotiation

The review workspace is built around **the agreement the other side provided**, **the posture of the represented party**, and **the decisions counsel records**.

## Review sequence

1. **Import source:** open a saved matter, paste contract text, or upload supported documents. Text-bearing PDF/DOCX/plain text can be extracted directly; scanned pages can require local OCR and manual correction.
2. **Choose posture:** indicate the party counsel represents, because the same clause can allocate advantage and risk differently for each side.
3. **Work the issue queue:** inspect the source clause, finding, severity, linked playbook guidance, assignment, comments and exceptions.
4. **Record a disposition:** approve; flag for negotiation; reject current wording; seek clarification; mark not applicable; accept a documented exception; or reopen, subject to the workflow's supported choices.
5. **Compare and redline:** examine the whole agreement, the relevant clause, a proposed redline, and textual changes between document versions.
6. **Close review:** resolve mandatory decisions and version confirmation requirements; verified findings can feed risk analysis.

## Why a review ledger is more useful than a chat answer

The source clause is retained as evidence of what was reviewed. The attorney's decision and rationale are retained as separate records. The workflow distinguishes **risk accepted** from **risk resolved**, and records completion against a specific document version. Ask Locke may explain a clause but does not record the attorney's approval for them.

## Example: customer MSA vs. vendor MSA

Counsel representing a vendor reviewing a customer's master services agreement may focus on uncapped liability, expansive indemnities, acceptance criteria, payment timing, termination rights, and IP ownership. Changing to the customer's posture can alter the issue emphasis and recommended negotiating positions. The lawyer can flag a provision, propose a redline, and retain that decision for subsequent negotiations.

## Firm playbooks

Organizations can maintain review rules, severities, enablement status, and preferred drafting guidance. Review findings can link back to the governing rule. This is a mechanism for consistency, **not a substitute for assessing the specific transaction**.
