# Governed guided drafting

Locke Professional starts with the transaction and the lawyer's objectives rather than treating each agreement as a blank prompt.

## Two entry paths

**Governed template:** Search the agreement library, inspect clause families, open a relevant template, complete the structured questions, and assemble from maintained terms.

**Custom-agreement brief:** Describe the parties, client objective, transaction, jurisdiction, desired negotiating posture, and known concerns; the workflow restates the brief, classifies the matter, flags risk signals, and presents issue cards and suitable production-cleared starting points.

## Why governance matters

1. **Guidance shaped by posture.** The same clause may affect different parties differently; drafting guidance is tied to who counsel represents.
2. **Issues before prose.** The product identifies important contractual choices and risk considerations before producing completed language.
3. **Maintained content.** Templates and clause families have release/readiness controls. An internal template record is not automatically a customer-ready template.
4. **Validation.** Assembly guards can flag load-bearing omissions, undefined terms, broken references, or incomplete jurisdiction coverage for review.
5. **Explicit AI provenance.** Where AI proposes structured language or assistance, the provider/model association is retained with the result rather than silently presented as library-authored text.
6. **Review handoff.** The completed draft can proceed to counterparty-style review, negotiation, approvals, and the contract repository.

## Example: a services agreement

A firm preparing a consulting services agreement records scope, payment, ownership, confidentiality, termination, and parties' roles. The guided workflow surfaces missing terms and proposes appropriate clause-family selections. Counsel evaluates the issues, edits the draft, and sends the reviewed language onward—without requiring a model to invent an entire unrestricted contract.

## Production library coverage

The documented public offering centers on nine collections: confidentiality and information protection; creative, marketing, and IP; construction and trades; employment and people; money and risk allocation; real estate and property; services and consulting; sales, purchasing, and supply; software and technology. The marketing count and signed-in count differ; [see the verification note](LIBRARY-COVERAGE.md).

**Boundary:** Assistance is not legal advice; a supported jurisdiction result or an explicit gap/uncertainty should be treated differently from a guaranteed statutory or local-form outcome.
